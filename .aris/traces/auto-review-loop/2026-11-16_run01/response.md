## Review: SPECTRA, ARIS W2 round 1 (interim)

**1. Contributions as I read them**
- An LLM writes a gm/Id "design-intent program" whose parameters are intent variables; a deterministic layer sizes devices through SKY130 LUTs, simulates, and feeds back prediction-vs-simulation discrepancies (`spectra/agent/spectra_agent.py`, `spectra/agent/dsl.py`).
- A device-level diagnosis (regions, margins, sensitivities, 17 rules) drives a refine/edit/declare policy, with CMA-ES in intent space and an adaptive worst-corner PVT loop (`spectra/agent/diagnose.py`, `spectra_agent.py:201-236`).
- SPECTRA-Bench: 92 certified + 25 designer + 6 control tasks over 12 families, 45-condition PVT verification, budget accounting (`scripts/build_bench.py`, `data/bench/*.jsonl`).

**2. Score and verdict**
Score: 5/10 as it stands. Verdict: not-ready. The engineering is well above the usual bar, numbers.tex is faithfully generated from the JSON, and budgets are hard-enforced (I checked all 307 E1 run files; none exceed 300/1200 sims or 12 calls). But the evidence currently supports the opposite of two of the three claimed contributions, and the benchmark's negative controls are broken. The path to 6.5 or 7 is clear and mostly uses compute you already planned.

### Weaknesses, ranked

**W1 (blocking). The headline comparison is not supported, and the robust-tier claim currently points the wrong way.** `E1_summary.json` tests: SPECTRA vs AnalogCoder-style p_holm 0.484 (3 wins, 1 loss over 26 tasks); vs Gen+TPE p_holm 0.085; vs SPECTRA-min p_holm 0.16. Nothing survives Holm. Worse, `intro.tex:37-39` advertises 83.3% on PVT-robust tasks while Table 3 shows the AnalogCoder-style agent at 100%. Those are 3 vs 2 tasks (tia-05, D07, comparator-05 vs tia-05, D07), and the one SPECTRA robust failure is a harness crash, not a design failure: `results/raw/E1/spectra/comparator-05/s1.json` has status "crash", "float division by zero" raised from `diagnose.report` at `spectra_agent.py:346`. The culprit is `diagnose.py:85`, which formats `sv / pv` when the program predicted exactly 0. Fix: guard `pv == 0`, re-run that task (LLM calls are cached, so cost is near zero), and never print a robust number from 3 tasks in the intro. Also, the four methods are being compared on different task sets right now (27/26/24/27 tasks; genbo lacks opamp-08/09 and comparator-05). Restrict every headline number to the common task set until E1 completes.

**W2 (blocking). The "oracle-unmet" control tier is broken, which undermines both the honesty claim and the benchmark contribution.** Two of six controls are trivially impossible by construction: C-amp-03 asks for 2.4 V swing on a 1.8 V supply (`control_tasks.jsonl` line 1; `build_controls.py:23` multiplies swing by 1.6), and C-filter-03 asks for peaking ≤ −5.8 dB (line 4; the measured peaking is clamped at ≥0 in `build_bench.py:59`, so subtracting 6 dB makes it unmeetable). Two others are solved by every method in every seed: C-cmirror-03 and C-comparator-02 (`results/E1_run_ctl.log`). So a 400-evaluation CMA-ES on the template is a weaker designer than either agent, and "oracle-unmet" says nothing about infeasibility. The Gate-1 review asked for ≥20 near-frontier controls and warned against "textbook-obvious" ones; neither happened. Minimum fix: regenerate controls by tightening only metrics with physical headroom, verify each control with a 2,000-evaluation oracle plus one SPECTRA and one direct run, and keep only tasks no method solves. Report SPECTRA's declare_infeasible behaviour on those. Until then, drop the control columns.

**W3 (blocking for C2). The fault-injection study currently shows that the diagnosis does not help, and it does not even test the diagnosis the agent uses.** `E4_summary.json`: top-1 74.2% with diagnosis vs 75.8% raw (−2 cases of 120); top-3 90.8 vs 89.2 (+2 cases). The per-class "helps on mirror ratios and short channels" claim (`experiments.tex:41-43`) rests on +2 cases of 21 and +2 of 10. Haiku drops 10 cases with the diagnosis. Moreover, `fault_injection.py:155-160` builds the "SPECTRA" evidence from +20% per-element sensitivities over every element with `pred=None`, whereas the agent uses +10% sensitivities on at most 8 intent variables (`spectra_agent.py:166-171`) and prediction discrepancies. E4 therefore never exercises the discrepancy feedback that the paper calls "the key design choice" (`method.tex:26`). Minimum fix: either reframe E4 honestly as a null result for localisation and make the E2 "w/o diagnosis" ablation the sole evidence for C2, or add an E4 arm that uses the actual agent diagnosis (intent-variable sensitivities from the agent's own program). Add binomial CIs to Table 4; most cells are within noise.

**W4 (major). Budgets are not identical, and the robust comparison is confounded.** Gen+TPE is hard-capped at 3 LLM calls (`genbo.py:43`, `rounds=3`); every genbo run has calls ∈ {1,3} while direct uses up to 12. The AnalogCoder-style agent uses one simulation per call, so its 300-simulation budget is decorative. For robust tasks Gen+TPE optimises three fixed corners and verifies PVT once per round (`genbo.py:100-106`) with no adaptive loop, whereas SPECTRA-min inherits `robust_stage`. So Gen+TPE's 25% robust result confounds parameterisation with PVT strategy. Minimum fix: let Gen+TPE regenerate until calls are exhausted, give it the same adaptive corner loop (or state plainly that the PVT loop is part of the method and show Gen+CMA-ES with and without it), and say in `experiments_setup.tex` that direct is call-bound by design.

**W5 (major). Benchmark saturation and circularity.** T1, T2 and designer tiers are at 100% for the AnalogCoder-style agent, and its median calls to success is 1. All discrimination comes from 9 T3 tasks (six op-amps, three TIAs). The designer tier is loose (D07, a "robust" op-amp, passes for direct on the first netlist, `direct/D07/s*.json` calls=1). Knowledge cards describe the exact template topologies (`knowledge.py:92-94` is the bgr template, `:106-107` the st6, `:86` the cm_wide) and are given to every method via `common.py:99`, so "choosing the topology itself" (`related.tex:31`) mostly means reading the card. The file header still says "SPECTRA-only" (`knowledge.py:67`). Also only 31 of the 58 T3/robust references were actually moved by frontier refinement (`"frontier_refined": true` count in tasks.jsonl); `bench.tex:26` implies all. Minimum fix: report the no-card arm for all methods (`spectra_noknow` already exists in `run_bench.py:29`), state the 31/58 number, and make T3+robust the pre-registered primary endpoint rather than overall success.

**W6 (major). Stale and unsupported numbers in the text.** See section 4.

**W7 (moderate). Seed protocol.** `analyze.py:18` discards completed s2 runs for genbo and spectra_min; `results/raw/E1/genbo/*/s2.json` exists for most tasks. Either include them or show the summary is insensitive.

**W8 (moderate). Statistics plan.** Wilcoxon with zsplit on per-task rates that are mostly 0 or 1 has little power; with ~27 tasks the CI half-width is about 10 points. Pre-specify: primary endpoint = pass@1 on T3 ∪ robust, cluster-bootstrap CIs over tasks, an exact paired permutation or sign test, and report per-task wins/losses. Report pass@3 too (SPECTRA's is 1.0).

**W9 (minor). Writing and template.** Abstract still says "[results pending]"; running head reads "Conference'17, July 2017"; `related.tex:20` asserts PANDA and Atelier verify at nominal only, which I could not confirm and which needs a citation check; "independent re-simulation" uses the same testbench and simulator (`run_bench.py:48-59`), so call it "re-simulation outside the agent's budget". The PDF is 7 pages with about 5.3 body pages, so there is room for E2/E3/E5, but the full-width Figure 1 should shrink.

**4. Numbers that do not match the result files**
- `experiments.tex:13-16` ("failures dominated by near misses"): `E1_failures.json` gives direct near_miss 2 of 6, median violation 0.099. And the file is stale: the raw directory now has 10 failing direct runs and 8 failing SPECTRA runs, versus n_fail 6 and 2 in the JSON. Regenerate before any failure narrative.
- `bench.tex:15` (157.7 kHz vs 159 kHz): no file in results/ contains it; the only "157.7" is a latency in llm_usage.jsonl. Violates the project's own rule.
- `method.tex:63` ("3–5× smaller than the raw W/L space"): no file.
- `method.tex:26-28` (68.7 dB / 33.4 MHz / 62°): matches `results/pilot/intent_prediction_vs_sim.json`, but that file does not record that the program was agent-written.
- `intro.tex:39` (83.3% robust): from 3 tasks, one of which is the crash above.
- `E1_summary.json` controls_declared 3 vs 4 declared_infeasible files on disk; summary is stale.
- Everything in numbers.tex, Table 3 and Table 4 matches the JSON.

**5. What most raises the score with the remaining compute**
1. Fix `diagnose.py:85`, re-run comparator-05, regenerate E1_summary and E1_failures, and restrict all tables to the common task set. Near-zero cost.
2. Finish E1 on T3 and robust tasks first, all families, before any more T1/T2/D runs; those tiers are saturated and only burn cores.
3. Run E2 "w/o diagnosis" and "w/o intent" on the 35-task subset with 3 seeds before E3 and E5. C1 and C2 live or die here.
4. Rebuild the control tier as in W2 and re-run SPECTRA and direct on it (two seeds each).
5. Run the no-card arm for SPECTRA and direct on the T3 subset.
6. Add the Gen+TPE fixes from W4 and re-run it on robust tasks only.
7. Write up D06 honestly: SPECTRA 1/3 vs direct 3/3 on the low-noise op-amp, because gm/Id programs do not predict flicker noise. A paragraph on where intent programs fail will do more for credibility than another percentage point.
